Introduction
Welcome to the TGH + USF Bariatric Center

Congratulations on your decision to change your life and invest in your health. At the TGH + USF Health Bariatric Center, we have specialized in weight-loss surgery since 1999 and offer every service you may need related to weight loss in one place.

Bariatric surgery has the ability to change your life — from treating conditions like diabetes, hypertension and sleep apnea to even reducing your cancer risk.

Bariatric surgery is just one tool. For best results, patients need to actively engage in nutritional, emotional and exercise counseling.

This handbook covers:

  • Bariatric surgery basics
  • Diet and nutrition
  • Exercise
  • Lifestyle changes
  • Clinic process
  • Overall tips for success

Process
The Pathway to Surgery
Bariatric Surgery is as SAFE as having your gallbladder removed, a hysterectomy, or elective hip or knee surgery.

The pathway takes six to eight months and includes multiple appointments with physicians, dietitians, psychologists, and exercise physiologists.

Monthly Timeline

  • Month 1: Surgeon Pre-Consult + start Nutrition Counseling
  • Month 2: Medical Evaluation, Psychology Evaluation, Labs
  • Months 1–6: Monthly Nutrition Counseling — missing even one requires restarting
  • Month 4: Case Review, Pre-Op Support Group, Exercise Physiology
  • Month 5: EGD
  • Month 6: Final Surgeon Consult → SURGERY ⭐
You must have NO WEIGHT GAIN during the program.
Requirements
Surgery Checklist

Nutrition Counseling

Monthly sessions with the Registered Dietitian — Months 1–6 minimum (up to 12 if required by insurance). Must be consecutive with no missed months.

Medical Evaluation

  • Lab work
  • Cardiology evaluation (if needed)
  • Pulmonology, Nephrology, Endocrinology (if needed)

Other Requirements

  • Nicotine cessation — off all products 90+ days before surgery
  • Psychology evaluation
  • Pre-Op Support Group (at least 1 session)
  • Exercise Physiology appointment
  • PCP letter of medical necessity
These are insurance requirement minimums. Authorization confirmed only after final paperwork submission.

Procedures
Surgical Options

Roux-en-y Gastric Bypass (RYGB)

Restrictive and malabsorptive. Pouch ~30–60 mL (2–4 tablespoons). Expected weight loss: 60–70% of excess weight.

Vertical Sleeve Gastrectomy

Restrictive. ~80% of stomach removed, banana-shaped sleeve of 60–120 mL. Expected weight loss: 50–70% of excess weight.

Duodenal Switch

Restrictive and malabsorptive. May be right for patients with severe diabetes. Surgeon will discuss if you are a candidate.

Gastric Band Removal

No longer placed. Existing bands will be removed and converted to sleeve or bypass if needed.

Health benefits include improvements in blood sugar, blood pressure, joint problems, breathing, mobility and overall function.
Goals
Weight-Loss Expectations

Expected Weight Loss

  • Gastric bypass: 60–70% of excess weight
  • Sleeve gastrectomy: 50–70% of excess weight

Excess body weight = current weight minus ideal weight for your height (BMI 25).

Keys to Success

  • Choose lean, high-protein foods
  • Limit high-calorie and starchy foods
  • Attend bariatric support group meetings
  • Exercise regularly
BMI requirements: BMI > 40, or BMI > 35 with a co-morbid condition.
Mental Health
Psychological Factors of Weight Loss

Mental Health

Obesity and mood symptoms like depression and anxiety are often intertwined. The psychologist will help address mental health and challenge negative thinking patterns.

Substance Use

No alcohol for at least one year after surgery. Tobacco products should never be used again. Higher risk of substance abuse post-operatively.

Eating Behaviors

Identifying hunger/fullness cues improves your relationship with food. Emotional eating will be addressed throughout the program.

Sleep

Inadequate sleep leads to weight gain, cognitive problems, low mood, and irritability. Healthy sleep habits will be discussed.

Pre-Op
Preparing for Surgery

Clinic Visits

Weight checked at every visit. Must see Registered Dietitian at least once a month. You are responsible for scheduling your own appointments.

Telemedicine

Telehealth appointments available. Most group sessions are via telehealth. Invest in a reliable home scale for telehealth visits.

Support Groups

At least one pre-op support group meeting required. Post-surgery support groups also available monthly.

Smoking

Must quit at least 90 days prior to surgery. Nicotine test required — must be negative to proceed.

Pregnancy

Wait at least 18 months after surgery. Use two forms of birth control for the first 18 months post-surgery.

Fees (if not covered by insurance)

  • Dietitian 1:1 — $50 | Class setting — $30
  • Psychologist initial — $100 | Follow-up — $50
Pre-Op
Changes in Medication

Diabetes Medications

Some may need to be stopped. After surgery, requirement for diabetes medication will decrease. Notify PCP if blood sugar falls below 100.

Diuretics

Most patients will not take these after surgery. Consider stopping while on the pre-op liquid diet if dizziness occurs.

Blood Thinners

Stop aspirin 10 days prior to surgery. Other blood thinners stopped at varying times — clarify with your surgeon.

NSAIDs (Motrin, Advil, Aleve)

Stop 14 days prior to surgery. Avoid after gastric bypass. Use Tylenol for pain instead.

Large Pills

Pills smaller than a Tic Tac or M&M are fine. Otherwise switch to liquid form.

After Surgery
Your Time in the Hospital

How You'll Feel

Groggy as anesthesia wears off. Some pain and bloating is normal — walking helps. Notify nurse of dizziness or blurry vision.

Drinking

1 oz of ice/water every 30 minutes once awake. No more than 3 oz per hour. Sip slowly — no gulping.

Walking

Walk at least 4 times a day. Start as soon as awake in recovery. Exercise feet and legs every hour even in bed.

Leg Compression Devices

Wear while sleeping or resting. Remove when walking or using the bathroom.

Deep Breathing

Use incentive spirometer every hour while awake — 10 slow deep breaths each time.

Discharge
Leaving the Hospital & Going Home

Length of Stay

Most patients spend 1–2 nights in the hospital.

Activity at Home

  • No lifting more than 20 lbs for 4 weeks (bypass) or 6 weeks (sleeve)
  • Stairs are okay
  • Drive only after 24 hours without pain medicine
  • First two weeks: walking and stationary bike only

Returning to Work

Most patients return at 3 weeks. Up to 6 weeks off allowed. Ask for return-to-work note at first post-op visit.

Recovery
After Surgery: Signs & Symptoms

Constipation

Start Miralax + Benefiber daily for 1 week. Add Senna if still constipated. Must be taken consistently for several weeks.

Nausea

Usually caused by dehydration. Take prescribed Zofran. Drink at least 64 oz of water daily. No carbonated beverages or straws.

Vomiting

Usually from eating too quickly or not chewing well. Take dime-sized bites. Call if vomiting with pain.

Incision Care

5–6 small incisions closed with sterile glue. Keep clean and dry. No soaking in baths or pools until fully healed.

Call (813) 844-7473 right away if you have:
  • Fever above 101°F
  • Increased redness or swelling at incisions
  • Cloudy or bloody drainage
  • Worsening pain, vomiting, shortness of breath, or heart palpitations
Nutrition
Nutrition Preparation

Pre-Op Checklist

  • At least 3 meals/day with lean protein at each
  • Avoid sugar, high-fat and fried foods
  • Avoid sugary and carbonated beverages
  • Eliminate caffeine (avoid 2 months post-surgery)
  • Eliminate alcohol (avoid 1 year post-surgery)
  • Drink at least 64 oz of water daily
  • Separate fluids from meals by 30 minutes
  • Chew food 20–30 times per bite
  • Start Bariatric Fusion Complete chewables (2/day before surgery)

Protein Sources

  • 3 oz chicken breast — 20g | Turkey breast — 26g
  • 3 oz tilapia — 22g | Salmon — 19g | Tuna — 22g
  • 1 egg — 7g | ½ cup cottage cheese — 15g
Supplements
Vitamins & Lifelong Supplementation
Take vitamin and mineral supplements every day for the rest of your life. Bariatric-specific supplements are required — standard vitamins are not adequate.

Bariatric Multivitamin with Iron

Bariatric Fusion® Complete Chewable — 4 tablets/day (2 morning, 2 night). Start 2/day before surgery. Chewable or liquid only.

Vitamin B12

1,000 mcg injections monthly starting 3 weeks post-surgery.

Calcium — 1,200–1,500 mg/day

Divided doses (max 500 mg at once). Choose brand with 400–800 IU vitamin D. Chewable or liquid only.

Iron — 45–60 mg/day

Take 2 hours apart from calcium. Take with vitamin C. Chewable form only.

Protein Goal

At least 60g protein daily. Per shake: ≤200 cal, ≥20g protein, <5g sugar.

Nutrition
Diet Progression After Surgery
DO NOT progress your diet earlier than recommended.
2 Weeks Before Surgery — PRE-OP DIET

Protein shakes for breakfast and lunch. Lean meat + 2 cups non-starchy vegetables for dinner. Nothing after midnight before surgery.

Surgery Day — CLEAR LIQUIDS

Water, sugar-free/non-carbonated/caffeine-free drinks, broth, sugar-free gelatin. 1–3 oz every 30 min. Goal: 64 oz daily.

Discharge – Day 21 — FULL LIQUIDS

Protein shakes, skim milk, low-fat Greek yogurt, soups without chunks. At least 60g protein daily.

Day 21–30 — PUREED DIET

Blended/liquefied foods (pudding consistency). 60–90g protein, 650–850 calories daily.

Day 31–45 — SOFT DIET

Well-cooked, moist, easily chewed foods. No dry meats, nuts, raw vegetables.

Day 46+ — BARIATRIC REGULAR DIET (Lifelong)

Low-fat, sugar-free. 90+g protein. Max 1,000 cal/day to 6 months, 1,200 cal/day to 1 year. Eat protein first, then vegetables, then carbs.

Dumping Syndrome

Rapid gastric emptying from sugary/fatty foods. Early (30–60 min): sweating, nausea, palpitations. Late (1–3 hours): low blood sugar, shakiness, fainting. Avoid simple carbs, sugary drinks, fried foods.

Fitness
Exercise Guidelines

Exercise is the most important factor in maintaining weight loss.

Weekly Goals

  • 20–30 min activity per day
  • 150 min moderate OR 75 min vigorous aerobic per week
  • Strength training all major muscle groups 2x/week
  • 300+ min/week to maintain weight loss
  • Stand every 30 minutes to reduce sitting time

First 2 Weeks Post-Op

Walking and stationary bike only. Walk as much as you feel able from day one at home.

The Exercise Physiologist at the Bariatric Center can help create your personal exercise regimen.
